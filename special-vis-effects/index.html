How Movies Brought Magic to Life

Special and Visual Effects Over the Years

Posted on October 21, 2026 by Gaebria Dunovan
All Posts

From the monstrous shark of Jaws (1975) to the creatures of Avatar (2009), visual and special effects (FX) have come a long way. Both practices date back to the late 19th century, but FX now versus then are practically in different worlds.

The 1902 French film, A Trip to the Moon, is widely considered to be a milestone in special effects, blowing audiences’ minds with what would now be considered rudimentary camera tricks and cheesily-painted backgrounds. So, how did we go from that to recreating the huge galaxies of space and giant creatures we used to only be able to dream of?

What Are Special and Visual Effects?

Special effects (SFX) and visual effects (VFX) are film techniques used to create things that would be impossible, dangerous, or too expensive to be done in real life. Although they are often used interchangeably, they’re actually rather different.

Visual effects are created in post-production (after the movie has been filmed) and include things like CGI, green screens, simulated physics (where complex things such as water, fire, or explosions are generated), and modern matte paintings, which are now often digital.

Special effects, on the other hand, are physical illusions filmed right on set. Examples of this are animatronics or puppets, machinery that simulates weather (such as snow machines), prosthetics and makeup, and props.

The Progression of Special and Visual Effects Through Movies


  • 1890s-1900s
    1. The Execution of Mary Stuart (1895)
      • Introduced stop-trick substitution, where the camera was stopped to replace the actor of Mary Stuart with a dummy before her head was "chopped off."
    2. A Trip to the Moon (1902)
      • Often considered the first science-fiction film
      • Introduced innovative, illusionary editing techniques with:
        • Trick photography
        • Superimposed images
        • Double-exposures
        • Dissolves and stop-motion jump cuts
        • Animation
        • The use of matte paintings
        • The substituation shot
        • Actors performing with themselves over split screens (also seen in Parent Trap, 1961)
        • Possibly the first miniature models ever utilized for film (one being a spaceship)
